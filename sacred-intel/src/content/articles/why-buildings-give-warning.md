The most dangerous myth about structural failure is that it comes without warning. In forensic investigation after investigation, the same finding recurs: the warnings were there, documented, and deferred.

## Corrosion is a clock

Reinforced concrete fails on a schedule you can often read. Once water and chloride reach the embedded steel, corrosion begins, the steel expands, and the surrounding concrete cracks — exposing more steel to more water. It's a feedback loop, and feedback loops accelerate.

## The signs, in plain language

- **Standing water** where it shouldn't collect, especially near columns and slabs.
- **Cracks that grow** — width and length changing between inspections.
- **Rust stains** bleeding through concrete surfaces.
- **Spalling** — concrete flaking or crumbling to expose rebar.

Any one of these has innocent explanations. Together, trending worse over time, they're a pattern engineers take seriously.

## Why warnings get missed

Rarely is it ignorance. More often it's economics and diffusion of responsibility: an inspection flags a problem, a repair is priced, a board or owner defers it, and the next inspection inherits a slightly worse version of the same finding. The physics keeps its own schedule regardless.

## The takeaway

If you take one thing from our Disaster Files work, let it be this: structural failure is a process, not an event. The event is just the last second of a story that was legible long before.
