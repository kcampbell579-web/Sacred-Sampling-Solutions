The all-clear after a chemical spill is not one statement — it's a set of judgments. Here are the five questions that tell you how solid it is.

## 1. What exactly was released?

You can't test for what you don't name. Ask which specific chemicals were involved, and whether any burned — combustion can create byproducts more concerning than the original cargo.

## 2. What was tested, and where?

A clean result three miles upwind says little about your street. Ask for the sampling locations and how close they were to affected homes, soil, and water.

## 3. Against what limit?

"Below the limit" only means something if you know the limit. Ask whether results were compared to health-based screening levels — and whether those levels assume short-term or long-term exposure.

## 4. What about private wells?

Municipal water gets tested systematically. Private wells often don't — the responsibility usually falls to the homeowner. If you're on a well, assume you need your own test.

## 5. Who's tracking the long tail?

The acute plume clears in hours. Soil residue and slow groundwater intrusion can last months. Ask who is responsible for follow-up monitoring, and for how long.

---

None of these questions assume bad faith. They're simply the difference between an all-clear that answers your question and one that answers a narrower one. Ask them out loud — the answers are usually public record.
