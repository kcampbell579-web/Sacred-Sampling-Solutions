When smoke turns a midday sky orange, the instinct is to react to what you can smell. But the real hazard is invisible: **PM2.5**, particulate matter under 2.5 micrometers across — small enough to bypass your body's defenses.

## Why size is everything

Your airways are built to trap large particles. PM2.5 defeats that system:

- It penetrates deep into the alveoli, the tiny sacs where oxygen enters the blood.
- The finest particles cross into the bloodstream itself, driving inflammation far from the lungs.
- Effects show up not just as coughing but as heart attacks and strokes in the days after a smoke event.

## Smoke doesn't stay local

One of the hardest lessons of recent fire seasons: smoke is a continental problem. Plumes ride the jet stream for **thousands of miles**, turning a fire in one region into an air-quality emergency in cities that never saw a flame.

> You can be a thousand miles from the nearest fire and still be breathing its most dangerous byproduct.

## What actually protects you

Not much folk wisdom survives contact with the data. What works:

- A well-fitted **N95 or P100** respirator — cloth and surgical masks do little against PM2.5.
- A **portable HEPA air cleaner** sized to the room, or a DIY box-fan filter in a pinch.
- Watching the **AQI** and treating "unhealthy" as a real signal to stay indoors, especially for kids, older adults, and anyone with heart or lung conditions.

## The bigger picture

Fire seasons are lengthening. Understanding PM2.5 isn't a one-week skill for a bad air day — it's becoming basic literacy for modern life.
