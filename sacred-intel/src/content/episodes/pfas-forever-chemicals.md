There's a reason PFAS earned the nickname **"forever chemicals."** The bond at the center of every one of these molecules — carbon locked to fluorine — is among the most durable in all of organic chemistry. It's what makes them brilliant at repelling water and grease, and it's exactly why they refuse to leave once they're in the environment.

## Where they come from

PFAS aren't one chemical. They're a family of thousands, engineered since the 1940s for a simple job: resist water, oil, and heat. That made them indispensable in:

- Nonstick cookware and stain-resistant fabrics
- Grease-proof food packaging
- Waterproof clothing and cosmetics
- **Aqueous film-forming foam** — the firefighting foam used for decades at airports and military bases

That last one matters. Firefighting foam is one of the clearest documented pathways from industrial use into the groundwater that communities drink.

## Why the health science moved fast

For years the story was "we're not sure." That's changed. Epidemiological work — much of it born out of a massive class-action study in the Ohio River Valley — has linked certain PFAS to elevated cholesterol, thyroid disruption, immune effects, and some cancers.

> The health goal for the two most-studied PFAS is effectively zero. That tells you how seriously regulators now take even small amounts.

## What the new EPA limits change

In 2024 the EPA finalized the first **enforceable federal drinking-water limits** for several PFAS, set in parts per *trillion*. Public water systems have to monitor, report, and reduce. For anyone on a private well, though, nobody is testing but you — which is where at-home water testing comes in.

## The bottom line

You can't undo the exposure that's already happened, and you shouldn't panic about it. What you *can* do is find out what's in your own water, choose a filter certified to remove *your* contaminants, and follow the regulatory story as it keeps moving — because it is moving quickly.
