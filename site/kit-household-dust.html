  • The relative percent composition of your household dust

Why test

This is the question almost everyone has wondered about and nobody has ever had answered. It is not a fear product — the appeal is curiosity, and it is genuinely informative.

A sample heavy in mineral particles points at tracked-in soil and a shoes-off conversation. Heavy in combustion particles points at cooking, candles or a road. Heavy in insect fragments points somewhere else entirely. None of it is a hazard finding, and all of it is useful.

How to collect your sample

Wipe a measured area of a horizontal surface that collects dust and is not cleaned often — the top of a bookshelf, a picture rail, the top of a door frame, the return grille of a heating system. Choose somewhere genuinely dusty: a thin sample produces a thin answer.

Do not sample visible mould growth with this kit. If a surface has visible growth on it, that is a different question and a different product.

How the lab analyzes it

The laboratory prepares the collected material on a slide and an analyst examines it under the microscope, identifying particle types by their shape and optical behaviour and estimating the proportion each type contributes to the whole (Dust Characterization Level 1).

Household dust is a genuinely mixed material, and the proportions vary enormously between homes — they say something specific about the building and how it is used.

What this test does not tell you
  • It identifies what the dust is made of. It does not measure lead, allergens, mould species or any chemical contaminant.
  • Results are proportions, not concentrations — a sample that is 40% mineral particles tells you about the mix, not how much dust is present.
  • There is no benchmark and none is needed. Nothing in a compositional report is a pass or a fail.
