Why it matters

Chloride is a taste-and-corrosion flag: elevated levels signal road-salt runoff, a failing water softener, or seawater/septic intrusion, and accelerate corrosion of pipes and fixtures (which can, in turn, mobilize metals).

Fluoride is protective for teeth at about 0.7 mg/L, but naturally high groundwater fluoride - or an over-adjusted supply - can cause dental and, at higher chronic levels, skeletal fluorosis. Pairing minerals with the full metals panel gives a fuller picture from one bottle.

How to collect your sample

Collect from a cold, unfiltered tap. Flush the line 2-3 minutes first, then fill the bottle without rinsing it (it is acid-preserved). Cap, chill on the gel pack, and ship the same day you collect.

How the lab analyzes it

Metals run by ICP-MS (EPA 200.8). Chloride and fluoride run by Ion Chromatography (EPA 300.0), which separates the anions on a column and measures each against calibrated standards - the same method the lab uses for drinking-water minerals.

Did you know
  • Private wells are not regulated by the EPA - testing is entirely the homeowner's responsibility, and most are never tested.
  • A salty or 'off' taste is often chloride - and can be an early sign your softener is failing or salt is reaching the water table.
  • Fluoride is one of the few drinking-water parameters with both a health benefit and a health risk depending on concentration.
