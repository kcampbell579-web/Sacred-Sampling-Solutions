Test Strips vs. Laboratory Water Testing

At-home test strips can give you a quick reading for the water quality indicators listed on the package. Mail-in laboratory testing measures the substances included in your selected kit and provides a written report. The right option depends on what you want to measure and how you plan to use the results.

When Test Strips Are Useful

Test strips can help with quick checks of indicators such as pH or hardness, when those indicators are listed on the package and an approximate range is enough. Follow the product’s directions and check its stated measurement range before interpreting the result.

When to Choose Laboratory Testing

If your concern is PFAS or low levels of lead, choose a kit that specifically lists those substances. Check what the test measures and the levels it can report. An accredited partner laboratory analyzes your sample using methods selected for the substances included in your kit.

What a Laboratory Report Includes

Your report shows which substances were tested, the results, and the units. It also includes applicable reference values and laboratory notes where relevant. A plain-language summary helps you understand the findings alongside the detailed results.

How to Choose a Water Test

Start with the substance or water quality indicator you want to measure. Review the kit’s listed coverage, sample collection instructions, and estimated laboratory turnaround. If you need results for a property transaction or another formal purpose, confirm the sampling and reporting requirements before ordering.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are water test strips accurate?

A strip’s performance depends on what it measures, its stated range, and how closely you follow the directions. Many strips show an approximate range based on a color change.

Can water test strips detect PFAS?

Check the specific product’s listed test coverage. For a PFAS concern, choose a test that identifies which individual PFAS compounds it measures and the levels it can report.

What does “Not Detected” mean on a lab report?

It means the laboratory did not find the substance at or above its reporting limit for that test.
