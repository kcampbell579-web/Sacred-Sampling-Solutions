What this means, in plain language

One result needs attention. Your nitrate came back at 11.4 mg/L, just above the EPA limit of 10 mg/L. Elevated nitrate usually points to fertilizer runoff, a nearby septic system, or animal waste reaching the water. It matters most for infants under six months and pregnant women. A recommended next step is to retest, consider a certified nitrate-removal system (reverse osmosis or ion exchange), and avoid using this tap for infant formula until it's resolved.

Everything else looks good. No bacteria or E. coli were detected, and chloride, fluoride, and pH are all within safe, normal ranges. Your water is on the harder side, which is a taste/scale issue — not a health one.

How to read it

Three columns, one clear answer.

1. Your result

The exact amount the lab measured in your water — a real number, not a color guess.

2. EPA limit (MCL)

The federal Maximum Contaminant Level — the health-based threshold your result is measured against. A “secondary” (SMCL) limit is about taste or appearance, not health.

3. Status flag

Below limit no action needed · Over limit worth attention · No health limit informational. Every over-limit result comes with plain-language next steps.

No science degree required. The report tells you what was found, whether it’s a problem, and what to do next — in plain English. Want the deeper version? Read Understanding your results.
